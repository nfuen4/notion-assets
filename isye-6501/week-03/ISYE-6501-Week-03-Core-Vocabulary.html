Scaling (Normalization) Data Scaling Click or press Enter to reveal
Bounded 0 to 1 range

Squishing all numbers in a column linearly so the smallest value becomes 0 and the largest value becomes 1.
Converting SAT scores (400 to 1600) onto a 0.0 to 1.0 scale.
Standardization (Z-Score) Data Scaling Click or press Enter to reveal
Mean 0 · Spread 1

Shifting a column so its average is 0 and measuring every value by how many “standard spreads” (standard deviations) it sits above or below average.
Saying a child’s height is “+2 standard deviations above average for their age.”
Point Outlier Outliers Click or press Enter to reveal
Extreme on its own

A single data point whose value is wildly far away from all other points on its own.
A human record listing Age = 240 years.
Contextual Outlier Outliers Click or press Enter to reveal
Bizarre in time or place

A value that isn’t unusual by itself, but is bizarre in its specific context (time or place).
A temperature of 88°F is normal in July, but a massive outlier in Boston in January.
Collective Outlier Outliers Click or press Enter to reveal
Abnormal group pattern

A group of points where no single point is extreme, but their combined pattern is abnormal.
A patient’s heart rate staying at exactly 72.000 beats per minute for 4 straight hours without fluctuating.
Grubbs’ Test Outliers Click or press Enter to reveal
Checks 1 extreme point at a time

A statistical test that checks whether the single most extreme value in a bell-shaped dataset is an outlier beyond random chance.
Testing whether the single slowest server response time in a batch of 500 pings is a statistical anomaly.
Change Detection Change Detection Click or press Enter to reveal
Spot permanent process shifts

Monitoring a stream of data over time to spot when the underlying process has permanently shifted.
Detecting that a factory bottling machine has slipped out of calibration and is under-filling bottles.
CUSUM (Cumulative Sum) Change Detection Click or press Enter to reveal
Running tab of persistent drift

A change-detection method that keeps a running tally of how far observations drift above (or below) average over time.
A “swear jar” that fills up whenever daily defects exceed normal tolerance until the jar overflows and triggers a maintenance inspection.
